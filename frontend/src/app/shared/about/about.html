About

Why DalTime exists.

A story about lifeguarding, scheduling chaos, and building something better.

The Founder

Hi, I'm Dallas LaComb.

I graduated from Central Connecticut State University with a degree in Computer Science. Before I wrote a line of production code, I spent around five years working as a lifeguard at the Meriden YMCA, and that experience is directly responsible for this application.

The Problem

Scheduling lifeguards is harder than it looks.

Lifeguards are part-time employees. Many are students with class schedules that change every semester. Every employee has to submit their availability (usually by text), and that availability changes month to month. The shifts that need to be filled change month to month too.

On top of that, there's a hard constraint that no part-time employee can go over 40 hours a week. The manager has to hold all of this in their head (or a spreadsheet) and build a schedule from scratch every month while leaving as little room for error as possible.

I watched this play out firsthand. When I asked my manager why we weren't using an app for this, the answer was simple: they didn't have the budget. Commercial scheduling software charges $3 or more per employee per month. For a nonprofit with dozens of part-time staff, that's a bill they can't justify.

The Solution

So I built it.

I took my experience with software engineering and AWS serverless architecture and set out to build a scheduling platform that costs virtually nothing to run, so it can be offered completely free to local nonprofit organizations.

DalTime is designed for organizations like the YMCA: community-focused, budget-conscious, and staffed largely by part-time employees who deserve a better experience than a text thread and a spreadsheet.

Managers get a structured tool that enforces constraints automatically. Employees get visibility into their schedules and a real way to submit availability. No one has to pay a per-seat licensing fee to make it happen.

Since its founding, DalTime has grown to serve all types of organizations. Approved nonprofits still receive free access, and all other organizations can get started on a free tier or choose a paid plan that fits their size.
